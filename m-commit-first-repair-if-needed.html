WHY THIS DEMO

M · Commit-First, Repair-If-Needed

eez-rollup0 doesn't wait for L1 before advancing L2. Every Sync block — the one block per L1 block that carries cross-chain effects — is committed to L2 the moment it's built, while its L1 postBatch submission is verified in the background by a separate task. If that submission doesn't land as expected, the L2 block is rolled back to its parent and rebuilt without it. This is optimistic composition: commit first, repair if needed — and it only works because L1, not L2, gets the final word on whether the block's effects actually happened.

What you should walk away understanding
  • The Sync block is never gated on L1 — it lands on L2 immediately, and a background task, not the sequencer, tracks whether its postBatch bundle actually settles.
  • A miss isn't silently absorbed: the composer reorgs the L2 head back to the Sync block's parent, rebuilds an empty sibling, and re-queues the batch's transactions for another attempt.
  • L2's own bookkeeping can be wrong — the Deriver's independent read of L1 is treated as the stronger proof and can override a false "failed" verdict; a follower can rebuild the whole L2 chain from L1 alone.

L1 IS THE SOURCE OF TRUTH — NOT L2
L2's own verdict can be wrong. The Deriver reads L1 independently, and its cursor only advances past a batch once check_claimed_state has accepted it — "a stronger settlement proof than the observer's log scan." That's why resolve_below_cursor can override a false Failed reading: "a false-negative observation must not undo a batch the Deriver confirmed."
And per the README: a follower can rebuild the identical L2 chain from L1 alone, no trust in the sequencer required. L2 only keeps what L1 confirms.
