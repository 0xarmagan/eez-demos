## 2. Scoring rule — none, and why

**This scorecard does not score pages on axes.** It reports a verdict, a count of open findings by severity, and the evidence for each. That is a decision, not an omission, and the reason is that the tracks do not ship the same kind of artifact.

The external audit of this site scored *"Runnable"* 2/2 for the Rust and proto pages, which ship no runnable artifact at all, while docking `q7`, `q4` and `pr5` for having no test. That is one word doing two jobs, and it moved the median. The axis has exactly two coherent readings:

- **"A reader can execute what is shown."** Then `pr1`-`pr4` score zero — there is no `cargo` in this repo, no Rust in `snippets/`, and nothing on those pages is executable by a reader. `ro1`-`ro4` score zero too: nothing on the operator track was run, by anyone, in this review or before it (`05-operator-pages.md` Method).
- **"The shown artifact is real."** Then untested Solidity scores full marks — `q7`'s snippet compiles standalone under `solc 0.8.28` (Task 9 §2), and `pr4`'s proto lines are byte-exact including column alignment (Task 4).

Under the first reading nine of fifteen pages score zero and the number says nothing about them. Under the second, "runnable" no longer means runnable. Averaging across three tracks that ship a Foundry test, a shell command and a Rust struct respectively manufactures a comparability that does not exist, and the resulting median is the artifact most likely to be quoted and least likely to be true.

**The one property that is comparable is reported instead of scored, because it is a fact and not a rating:** *was this page's central claim checked by executing something?*

| Track | Executed | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Dapp (`q3`, `q5`, `q6`) | **Yes** | `forge 1.7.1`, 13/13 green, pasted in `09-runnables.md` §3 |
| Dapp (`q2`, `q4`, `q7`) | Compiled, not tested | 12/12 files compile standalone; no test exists |
| Researcher | **No** | Read against upstream at the pin. Nothing compiled, no `cargo` |
| Operator | **No** | Desk-check against upstream docs. No Docker, no Kurtosis, no RPC |

Two severity labels are used identically across all three tracks, from the skill's own vocabulary: **BLOCKING** = breaks in production, leaks value, or teaches a security-relevant falsehood. **ACCURACY** = a wrong name, field, or claim that does not meet that bar. Those are the two columns in §1, and nothing else is counted.

### Credit where the review found it

- **`q5` is the most rigorously grounded page on the site, and its digests are now confirmed five independent ways** — pycryptodome + a hand-built ABI encoder; a hand-written Keccak-f[1600] (written from scratch because `hashlib.sha3_256` is NIST SHA-3, not keccak, and would have silently mismatched); `cast abi-encode` + `cast keccak`; the shipped snippet deployed to a live anvil and read back with no `require` in the path; and a raw-preimage probe proving solc's own `abi.encode` emits byte-identical 320 bytes. The test's claim to *"pin solc's output against an independent implementation"* is now verified in the direction it was always asserting and never checking.
- **`q6`'s test refuses to fake protocol state and says so in writing** (`Q6ManagerDirect.t.sol:17-21`): faking the positive case *"would mean either adding a setter that the real contract does not have, or poking storage with `vm.store`. Both would be testing the fake rather than the protocol."* That instinct, written where a reader will copy it, is the standard the `q8` candidate arrived failing and is now being rebuilt to meet.
- **`pr5` is the only page on the researcher track with zero annotation defects, and the reason is structural, not care:** it pins its derivation at a SHA in the snippet header and is drift-checked against `snippets/q1-compute-address.sol`. Every other page on that track writes its `// file:NNN` annotations by hand into a surface no checker reads — and four of them are wrong. That is the pattern the other four should copy.
- **No atomicity overclaim exists anywhere on the site.** The only `atomic` string in 17 files is `aria-atomic="true"`. For a synchronous-composability project this is the easiest claim to overreach on.
